# Devtool Positioning

## When to Use

- A devtool client is launching, rebranding, or struggling to explain what they do.
- Marketing content keeps drifting because there is no shared positioning anchor.
- Sales and DevRel tell different stories about the same product.

## Core Principles

1. **Positioning before content**: every blog post, demo, and tweet traces back to one positioning statement.
2. **One developer persona at a time**: "developers" is not a persona — name the specific person and their job.
3. **Differentiate on a job, not a feature**: lead with the outcome the developer can't get elsewhere.
4. **Evidence over adjectives**: claims need proof (benchmarks, a repo, a customer story), not "best-in-class".
5. **Small test before broadcast**: validate the message on a small audience before scaling the spend.

## Procedure

1. **Intake**: from the client, capture the product, current pitch, and the 3 competitors they lose deals to.
2. **Persona**: name one primary developer persona — role, context, trigger event, current workaround.
3. **Positioning statement**: fill the template (see below).
4. **Messaging hierarchy**: one anchor message + 3 supporting pillars, each with proof.
5. **Pressure test**: put the statement in front of 3–5 real developers (or a community proxy); revise on confusion.

## Templates

### Positioning statement

> For **[persona]** who **[context / trigger]**, **[product]** is a **[category]** that **[outcome]**. Unlike **[competitor / status quo]**, we **[differentiator with proof]**.

### Messaging hierarchy

- **Anchor**: one sentence the whole market should remember.
- **Pillar 1** — [claim] · proof: [benchmark / repo / case]
- **Pillar 2** — [claim] · proof: [benchmark / repo / case]
- **Pillar 3** — [claim] · proof: [benchmark / repo / case]

### Persona card

- **Who**: [role, seniority, stack]
- **Trigger**: the moment they go looking for a solution
- **Today's workaround**: what they do without you
- **Win condition**: the outcome that makes them stay

## Gotchas

- **A positioning statement nobody inside the client can repeat is worthless.** If the founder can't say it in one breath, it's not done — simplify until it fits.
- **Don't position against a competitor the developer hasn't heard of.** "Unlike obscure-tool-X" means nothing; position against the status quo (a bash script, a manual process) they actually use.
- **Persona drift kills the whole funnel.** If the persona is "all developers", the content will resonate with none. Pick one and let other segments come later.
- **Proof that can't be verified will be challenged publicly.** A benchmark with no reproducible method invites a Hacker News takedown. Use real, citable numbers or frame as illustrative.
- **Don't let the client's internal jargon leak into external messaging.** "Our synergistic orchestration layer" means nothing to a developer deciding whether to try it. Translate to the job they're hiring you for.

## Checklist

- [ ] Is there one positioning statement the client's founder can say in one breath?
- [ ] Is the primary persona named, not "developers"?
- [ ] Does every claim have a proof pointer?
- [ ] Has the statement survived a test with real developers?

## Shared Context

- Outcomes over output: we measure whether developers adopt, not whether they liked the post.
- Evidence-based: cite real signals and proof; never invent metrics or claims.
- Developer respect: no hype, speak their language, never fake "best".
- AI is a lever, not autopilot: AI drafts and analyzes; a human owns the message and the judgment.
